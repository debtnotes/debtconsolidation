# Resolution paths and bankruptcy

## Compare the paths before choosing one

| Path | Upside | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| Card hardship program | May reduce rate/payment without a new lender. | Cards may close; written payment still must fit the essentials-first budget. |
| Nonprofit debt-management plan | One payment and sometimes lower interest. | Usually full repayment and regular payment; fees may apply even if initial counseling is free. |
| Temporary no payment | Preserves essentials while facts/advice are gathered. | Credit damage, late fees, collections, and suit risk. |
| Direct settlement | May reduce total paid when cash is truly available. | No guarantee; tax consequences may arise; require a signed written settlement/release before payment. |
| Attorney-negotiated settlement | Counsel can evaluate defenses, releases, judgment risk, and timing. | Fees; no guaranteed creditor acceptance. |
| Sell or downsize | Can remove debt/housing costs if equity is enough. | Moving, liens, sale cost, and SSI home-sale proceeds need advance review. |
| HELOC or home-equity loan | Could lower interest and use a familiar lender. | Converts unsecured debt into home-secured debt; may be variable-rate and foreclosure-capable. |
| Reverse mortgage (usually 62+) | Can alter required mortgage payment and access equity. | Balance grows; taxes/insurance/maintenance remain; estate, benefits, and counseling issues. |
| Bankruptcy | Can resolve unsecured debt and stop collection through the automatic stay. | Home equity, title, recent transactions, and filing type require individual counsel. |

## Chapter 7 and Chapter 13

**Chapter 7** can discharge ordinary unsecured card debt, subject to exceptions and recent-transaction scrutiny. Social Security benefits are generally excluded from the means-test current-monthly-income calculation, but assets, expenses, income, and local practice still must be fully disclosed. If there is material nonexempt equity, a trustee may sell a home and distribute value after liens, sale costs, and exemptions.

**Chapter 13** commonly uses a three-to-five-year plan. It must be feasible and normally give unsecured creditors at least what they would receive in a Chapter 7 liquidation (the best-interests/liquidation-value test). If disability income leaves no real surplus, feasibility can be difficult. Judicial liens can sometimes be avoided to the extent they impair an exemption; lien type and timing matter.

Maryland’s § 11-504(f) now provides, in bankruptcy, an owner-occupied residential exemption tied to the federal § 522(d)(1) amount, subject to statutory limits. The filing-date dollar amount should be confirmed by counsel. It is not equal to the house’s value and not a general non-bankruptcy collection shield. [Maryland statute](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gcj&enactments=false&section=11-504).

## Equity illustrations only

Assume a $400,000 home, sole ownership, one mortgage, no selling costs or other liens. These are not legal conclusions.

| Mortgage balance | Gross equity before costs/exemptions | Meaning |
|---:|---:|---|
| $0 | $400,000 | Requires immediate attorney analysis; high equity is not presumed safe in Chapter 7. |
| $200,000 | $200,000 | Ownership, sale costs, exemption, and liens can still change the recoverable amount substantially. |
| $300,000 | $100,000 | Still may be material after costs. |
| $375,000 | $25,000 | Costs and exemption may leave little, but an attorney must calculate it. |

The Maryland Bankruptcy Court cautions that self-represented Chapter 13 cases are more often dismissed and less often discharged than represented cases. [Court guidance](https://www.mdb.uscourts.gov/dont-have-a-lawyer).

## Questions for a bankruptcy/consumer lawyer

1. What is the actual net equity after payoff figures, title share, liens, sale costs, and the applicable filing-date exemption?
2. Is Chapter 7, Chapter 13, negotiated settlement, or sale/downsize least risky to the home?
3. Would any recent gift, transfer, cash advance, insider repayment, refinancing, or new lien create a problem?
4. Can a judicial lien be avoided? Does tenancy by the entirety affect the analysis?
5. What is the safest way to buy time while preserving essential living costs?
