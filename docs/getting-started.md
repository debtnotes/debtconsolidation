# Start here — facts, safety, and budget

## This week’s stoplight

| Do now | Pause and check | Do not do without independent advice |
|---|---|---|
| Pay housing, utilities, food, medicine, insurance, and taxes before unsecured cards. | Ask card issuers’ hardship departments for written options only. | Put a friend on the deed, sign a deed of trust, refinance, take a HELOC, or grant a lien. |
| Open every letter. Calendar a summons, hearing, and response deadline. | Gather benefit notices, bank statements, card statements, deed, mortgage/HELOC statements, and court papers. | Transfer the house, add a co-owner, repay an insider, or make large gifts before legal/benefits review. |
| Keep direct-deposited benefits in a separate account and save statements. | Get both a nonprofit credit-counseling view and a Maryland consumer/bankruptcy screening. | Accept a lump sum into an SSI recipient’s account without benefits timing/reporting guidance. |

## The facts worksheet

| Topic | Record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Benefits | SSDI / SSI / both / unknown; net monthly amount; direct-deposit account | SSI has strict income/resource rules; SSDI does not use the same resource test. |
| Other programs | Medicaid, SNAP, housing aid, Medicare Savings Program | Each can have different reporting/resource rules. |
| Home and title | county, deed, sole/joint/TBE ownership, primary residence | Ownership changes creditor and bankruptcy analysis. |
| Liens and equity | mortgage, HELOC, taxes, HOA, reverse mortgage; payoff figures; sale costs | **Net equity = value − payoff balances − estimated sale costs.** |
| Cards | creditor, balance, rate, minimum, last payment, status, whose name | Determines liability, limitation questions, settlement options, and lawsuit risk. |
| Court status | collection letter / suit / judgment / lien / garnishment; case number and deadline | A judgment or court deadline cannot be safely ignored. |
| Recent transactions | transfers, cash advances, insider repayments, gifts, refinance/new lien | These can create bankruptcy, SSI/Medicaid, and tax issues. |

## Survival-budget worksheet

Do not count hoped-for friend help, an expected settlement, or future sale proceeds as dependable income.

| Income and essentials | Monthly amount |
|---|---:|
| SSDI/SSI after deductions | $ |
| Other dependable income | $ |
| **Total dependable income** | **$** |
| Mortgage/HOA/taxes/home insurance | $ |
| Utilities/phone/internet | $ |
| Food and household | $ |
| Medical, prescriptions, care, transportation | $ |
| Home-repair reserve and unavoidable costs | $ |
| **Essential costs** | **$** |
| **Safety buffer** | **$** |
| **Sustainable debt-payment ceiling** | **$** |

If the final number is zero or negative, a debt-management plan, HELOC, or private amortizing loan is not actually affordable. Ask a professional to compare hardship, temporary nonpayment, settlement timing, sale/downsize, and bankruptcy instead.

## Bring this to an appointment

- Benefit award letters; 12 months of bank statements; list of every public-benefit program.
- Deed, latest mortgage/HELOC statements, property-tax and HOA statements, and a credible value estimate.
- Card statements, collection letters, settlement offers, credit reports, and all court papers.
- Tax returns, completed budget, and a timeline of recent large transactions.
