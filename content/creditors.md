# Maryland collection, judgments, and the home

## Collection letters versus court papers

A card issuer or collector can seek payment and may sue. A court judgment—not a routine collection letter—is generally the gateway to garnishment and a judgment lien. Preserve all notices and respond by the deadline. The CFPB describes the federal right to request debt-validation information from a collector and to dispute it: [CFPB debt collection](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-debt-collection-en-329/).

Maryland’s ordinary civil limitations period is generally three years, but the accrual date, contract terms, choice-of-law, payment history, and existing judgment all require case-specific analysis. A limitations defense does not erase the debt; it must be timely raised. Do not make a token payment or sign an acknowledgment merely to “test” the issue. [Md. Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 5-101](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcj&enactments=false&section=5-101).

## A judgment has a long tail

In Maryland, a judgment generally lasts 12 years and may be renewed for another 12. [§ 5-102](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcj&enactments=false&section=5-102); [Maryland Courts guide](https://www.mdcourts.gov/courthelp/judgmentsanddebtcollection). A recorded lien can complicate or consume proceeds from a future sale, refinance, inheritance, or estate administration.

This does not make a forced home sale automatic. Procedure, equity, exemption, existing liens, and ownership determine what can happen. It does mean the house must be part of the analysis.

## Ownership is not a detail

Confirm the deed. Sole ownership, joint tenancy, tenancy in common, and married ownership as tenants by the entirety can change who owns what interest and what a creditor of one versus both spouses may reach. Tenancy by the entirety can be especially important where only one spouse owes the debt, but joint debt, a title change, death, divorce, and bankruptcy facts may change the analysis. Bring the deed and actual contracts to counsel.

Maryland’s general exemption includes up to $6,000 in cash or property under § 11-504(b)(5), alongside other exemptions. The newer owner-occupied residential exemption in § 11-504(f) is expressly a **bankruptcy** exemption; it is not a blanket defense to ordinary judgment collection. [Md. Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 11-504](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gcj&enactments=false&section=11-504).

## If a bank account is frozen

1. Read the garnishment notice and court deadline immediately.
2. Preserve statements showing direct-deposited benefits and any other legally exempt source.
3. Use the Maryland exemption procedure promptly; automatic federal protection does not eliminate all follow-up work.
4. Seek Court Help or a lawyer if the account includes more than protected direct deposits or the debt has special status (tax, federal debt, child support, or alimony).
