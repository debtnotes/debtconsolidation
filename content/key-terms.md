# Key terms

For the terms most likely to change the decision, use the linked [key-term guides](./terms/).

## Automatic stay

In bankruptcy, a legal pause that generally stops most collection activity while the case is pending. It has exceptions and does not solve the separate question of whether a home has nonexempt equity.

## Bona fide loan

A genuine, legally enforceable loan made in good faith. For SSI, this distinction can affect whether funds are treated as income, although money retained into the next month may still count as a resource. See [benefits](./benefits/).

## Charge-off

An accounting decision by a creditor that generally means it treats an account as a loss. It does **not** erase the debt or prevent collection/suit.

## Current monthly income / means test

Bankruptcy concepts used to assess Chapter 7 eligibility and related issues. Social Security benefits are generally excluded from the means-test current-monthly-income calculation, but benefits and all other information still must be accurately disclosed in the case.

## Debt consolidation

Combining or replacing debts—such as with a new loan, balance transfer, or one-payment program. A consolidation loan can be unsecured or secured by a home; the latter creates materially more housing risk.

## Debt management plan (DMP)

A structured repayment plan, often offered through a nonprofit credit-counseling organization. It commonly seeks lower interest rates or waived fees but usually repays the debt in full and may charge plan fees. It is distinct from debt settlement.

## Debt settlement

An effort to persuade a creditor to accept less than the total owed. It can include fees, missed payments, collection/lawsuit risk, credit harm, and possible taxable cancellation-of-debt income. See [scam and settlement screening](./scams/).

## Deed of trust / mortgage

A document that creates a lien on real property to secure a loan. In Maryland, it can give the lender foreclosure remedies if the borrower defaults. Never use a generic form for a home-secured private loan.

## Direct deposit / protected amount

Federal rules require an account review after certain garnishment orders and leave a calculated amount of qualifying directly deposited federal benefits accessible. It is not a permanent or unlimited protection for all funds in an account.

## Exemption

A law that protects specified property or value from certain creditor claims. Exemptions are context-specific: Maryland’s general exemption and bankruptcy exemptions are not identical, and protection does not automatically eliminate a lien.

## Judgment lien

A lien arising from a court judgment that may attach to real property when properly recorded. It can complicate a sale, refinance, or estate even if it does not cause an immediate forced sale.

## Net equity

The likely value left after subtracting mortgages, HELOCs, tax/other liens, and realistic sale costs from a home’s market value. It is the starting point—not the final answer—for home-risk analysis.

## Secured debt

A debt backed by collateral, such as a mortgage or a friend’s recorded deed of trust. If payments fail, the creditor can pursue the collateral under applicable law. Credit-card debt is usually unsecured.

## Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)

Federal disability insurance generally based on a worker’s earning record. It is not subject to SSI’s ordinary resource limit, but its benefits are still relevant to budget, bank protections, other programs, and bankruptcy disclosure.

## Supplemental Security Income (SSI)

A federal needs-based benefit for aged, blind, or disabled people with limited income and resources. Gifts, retained cash, loan proceeds, and property transfers can affect eligibility. A person can receive both SSI and SSDI.

## Tenancy by the entirety (TBE)

A form of property ownership available to married couples in Maryland. It can significantly affect creditor and bankruptcy analysis when only one spouse owes a debt, but the deed, debt ownership, title changes, and facts must be reviewed individually.

## Trustee

In bankruptcy, the person appointed to administer the case. In a Chapter 7 case, a trustee may evaluate and potentially sell nonexempt assets for creditors.

## Unsecured debt

Debt without a specific collateral lien, such as most credit-card debt. It can still lead to a lawsuit, judgment, bank restraint, or judgment lien; it is not automatically harmless.
