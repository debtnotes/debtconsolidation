# Disability income, SSI, and Medicaid

## Income protection is real, but it is not the whole analysis

For ordinary private card debt, [42 U.S.C. § 407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/407) protects Social Security benefits from execution, levy, attachment, and garnishment. SSI has parallel protection. Federal account rules require a bank served with a qualifying garnishment order to review direct deposits during the two-month lookback period and leave the calculated protected amount accessible without a pre-claim. [31 C.F.R. § 212.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/212.6).

The automatic amount is not unlimited tracing protection. Funds above it, funds without qualifying direct-deposit traceability, and commingled non-benefit funds can still be frozen while the person asserts an exemption. Keep statements and the award letter. Maryland Courts explains bank-account exemption requests and identifies Social Security as a possible exempt source: [Judgments & debt collection](https://www.mdcourts.gov/courthelp/judgmentsanddebtcollection).

**Practical arrangement:** have benefits directly deposited to an account used only for those benefits. Avoid mixing gifts, wages, loan proceeds, or friends’ money into that account.

## SSDI versus SSI

| Question | SSDI | SSI |
|---|---|---|
| Basic program | Disability insurance benefit based on work record. | Needs-based disability benefit. |
| Asset/resource limit | No SSI-style resource limit. | Generally $2,000 for an individual and $3,000 for a couple. |
| Why a lump sum matters | May affect other programs/taxes but not SSDI eligibility itself. | A gift can be income in receipt month; retained money can be a resource next month. |
| Loan proceeds | Still consider other benefits/taxes/creditors. | A bona fide loan is not income, but unspent proceeds are a countable resource from the next month. |

### The direct impact of disability and Social Security on this debt situation

| Issue | SSDI | SSI | Both / any disability recipient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the disability benefit itself make credit-card debt disappear? | No. The debt remains a contractual obligation unless settled, discharged, or otherwise resolved. | No. | Disability can make a payment unaffordable, but it does not by itself cancel the debt. |
| Can a private card creditor garnish the benefit payment? | The benefit has strong federal protection under § 407. | SSI has parallel protection. | Federal tax debts, certain federal debts, child support, and alimony have different rules; get immediate advice if relevant. |
| Does direct deposit help? | Yes—bank rules create automatic protection for a calculated amount in the two-month lookback period. | Yes. | Keep benefits in a separate account and save statements; the automatic protection is not unlimited tracing protection. |
| Does a cash gift matter? | SSDI is not means-tested, though tax/other-program issues may remain. | It can be income in the month received and a countable resource if retained. | Medicaid, SNAP, housing support, and Medicare Savings Programs have their own rules. |
| Does a bona fide loan matter? | Not an SSDI resource issue in the SSI sense, but it creates repayment and creditor risk. | Not income if legally enforceable; cash retained into the next month can count as a resource. | A loan secured by the home also creates foreclosure risk, regardless of benefit type. |
| Does bankruptcy need to disclose it? | Yes—disclose all income, benefits, assets, and accounts accurately to counsel/court. | Yes. | Social Security’s treatment in the Chapter 7 means test differs from ordinary income, but a lawyer must assess the full case and home equity. |

**Concurrent SSDI/SSI is a high-caution branch.** It means the SSDI payment may be low enough that SSI supplements it; the SSI income/resource rules still matter. Do not assume that receiving “Social Security disability” means only SSDI rules apply—verify the award letter or SSA account before accepting a gift, loan, settlement fund, sale proceeds, or any title change.

**Public benefits and debt settlement:** settling a card debt is not automatically an SSI resource problem, but the path to settlement can be. A friend’s cash, loan proceeds, retained settlement fund, home-sale proceeds, or a property transfer can change SSI/Medicaid eligibility or create reporting duties. The safest first move is usually to pay for benefits/legal screening rather than deposit a large lump sum into the recipient’s account.

SSA says a loan must be enforceable under state law. Its 2026 consumer guidance states that a valid loan does not reduce SSI as income, while borrowed funds retained into the next month count toward the resource limit. [SSA: Spotlight on Loans](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/spotlights/spot-loans.htm); [POMS cash-loan policy](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/%20lnx/0501120220).

## Home, sale proceeds, and transfers

The home occupied by the recipient is generally an excluded SSI resource, but selling it, adding someone to title, giving away an ownership share, or retaining proceeds all create separate questions. A transfer for less than fair market value can create SSI ineligibility unless an exception is established. [SSA POMS: transfers](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/%20lnx/0501150001).

For an excluded home sale, planned replacement-home funds have a limited exclusion generally requiring use within three full calendar months after the month of receipt. [SSA POMS: home replacement funds](https://secure.ssa.gov/POMS.NSF/lnx/0501130110). Medicaid, SNAP, and housing programs can differ. Treat a money or title change as a hard stop for a benefits counselor or administering agency before closing.

## Questions to take to a benefits professional

1. What exact benefit is being received, and what must be reported before a gift, loan, settlement, sale, or title change?
2. Does the proposed arrangement create income, a countable resource, a transfer penalty, or a reporting deadline?
3. Would paying a professional/provider directly be handled differently than giving cash?
4. What should be preserved to prove direct deposit, source, and spend-down?
