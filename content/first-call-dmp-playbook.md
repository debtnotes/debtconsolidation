# Maryland nonprofit DMP first-call playbook

**Reviewed August 17, 2026.** A first call should obtain information and a written quote—not commit the caller to a plan. No script can **assure** the best terms: creditor participation, interest concessions, fees, and eligibility are account- and state-specific. This playbook helps a Maryland caller compare two providers safely and avoid accepting a plan that does not fit the real budget.

> **The goal of call one:** “I want free counseling and a written Maryland DMP quote. I am not enrolling or giving payment instructions today.” CFPB says a reputable counselor should provide free service information before requiring details about the caller’s situation, and says to confirm that creditors accepted a proposed DMP before sending payments to the counseling organization. [CFPB: credit counseling](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/).

## Before calling: prepare a one-page fact sheet

Use rounded figures for a first screening, then verify them from statements before enrollment.

| Bring / know | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Net reliable monthly income; note SSI, SSDI, both, or other income | Tests whether any payment leaves essentials and a safety buffer. No medical diagnosis is needed for a first screening. |
| Housing, utilities, food, medicine, insurance, taxes, transport, and essential-care costs | These come before unsecured-card payments. |
| Every unsecured debt: creditor, approximate balance, APR/minimum, current/late/collection/lawsuit status | A plan that excludes a material account may not solve the problem. |
| Court case number and deadline, judgment, garnishment/freeze, lien, foreclosure, or bankruptcy consultation | A DMP does not answer court papers or give legal advice. |
| Target payment that survives a bad month | Use the [survival budget](./getting-started/)—not a hoped-for gift, sale, or overtime. |

Do **not** give a full Social Security number, bank/card credentials, online-banking password, verification code, remote device access, ACH authority, or e-signature merely to obtain a first quote. See the [first-call privacy FAQ](./faq/#first-call-with-mmi-or-greenpath-what-should-i-not-reveal).

## Safest way to make initial contact

1. **Start the contact yourself.** Do not respond to an unexpected call, text, ad, search-result phone number, or social-media message. FTC warns that unexpected debt-relief contacts seeking financial/personal information may be scams. [FTC warning](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/03/looking-debt-relief-heres-how-avoid-scam).
2. **Use the provider’s official page, then independently verify the web address.** Bookmark it; do not follow a link supplied by an unsolicited caller.
3. **Ask for counseling, not enrollment.** Request a scheduled, unrushed session if you cannot safely complete the review in one call. GreenPath says its initial counseling is free and no appointment is required, but a scheduled call is reasonable; both providers’ published contact details can change.
4. **Make two comparable calls.** Use this same script with MMI and GreenPath. Do not tell either that it has “won” your business.
5. **Move to a secure channel only when needed.** Ask why each sensitive item is needed, whether a credit report will be pulled, who can access/share it, and whether the action is optional. Get the privacy notice first.

## Read-aloud script for the first call

> “Hello. I live in Maryland and I am seeking **free nonprofit credit counseling** and a written quote for a **Debt Management Plan** for eligible unsecured debts. I am comparing options and I am **not authorizing enrollment, creditor contact, a credit pull, an ACH withdrawal, or any payment today**.
>
> My essentials-first budget leaves about **$___ per month at most** for unsecured debt after housing, food, medicine, utilities, insurance, taxes, and a small buffer. I have approximately **$___** in unsecured debt across these creditors: **[list]**. [If true: I also have court papers/case number **___** and a deadline of **___**; I understand you do not provide legal representation.]
>
> Please tell me whether this is a standard DMP, debt resolution/settlement, or any other product. I only want a DMP quote today. Please send the complete Maryland terms in writing, including the accounts you can include, projected payment and payoff date, every fee, each creditor’s expected APR/concession, cards that would close, what happens after a late payment, and what I must do until every creditor accepts.
>
> Before I share sensitive information, please send your privacy notice, NMLS/license information, whether you will pull my credit, what consent you are asking for, how creditor contributions or employee incentives work, and the complaint/escalation process. I will review the written information and call back if I decide to proceed.”

### If pressured to enroll, use this response

> “I do not make financial commitments on a first call. Please email or mail the terms and your privacy notice. I will compare them with another nonprofit counselor and verify participation with my creditors. Please note that I do **not** authorize a payment, ACH debit, electronic signature, credit pull, or creditor contact today.”

Walking away from a pressured call is appropriate. CFPB advises getting fees in writing, reading any agreement before signing, and avoiding an organization that pushes a DMP before analyzing the financial situation. [CFPB selection questions](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/).

## The questions that make the terms comparable

Ask each provider the exact same questions. “We work with most creditors” is not an answer about a named account.

### Product and eligibility

- Is the recommendation a **standard DMP** that repays the eligible balance, a debt-resolution/settlement program, counseling only, or a loan referral? Put the product name in the written quote.
- Which named accounts are included, excluded, ineligible, charged off, or already in litigation? Is the plaintiff/collector included?
- Does the provider need the original creditor’s acceptance for each account? Who will confirm it, and when?

### Total cost and payment mechanics

- What is the first payment date, ongoing monthly payment, setup fee, monthly fee, cancellation/default fee, total estimated fees, and estimated final payment date?
- For each account, what balance, APR, minimum/concession, and payoff month does the quote assume? What portion is provider fee versus creditor payment in month one?
- Are creditor contributions received? How are they calculated? Are employees paid more if a caller enrolls? MMI and GreenPath both publish disclosures that address creditor contributions; obtain the current written version for the actual product. [MMI disclosure](https://www.moneymanagement.org/disclosures); [GreenPath counseling disclosure](https://portal.greenpath.com/Resources/AddingaFinancialPartnerEN.pdf).
- Which cards will close? Is any emergency-card exception truly permitted by the relevant issuer, in writing?

### Failure, court, and exit rules

- What happens after one late, short, returned, or missed payment? Which creditors can withdraw concessions, resume collection, or pursue legal action?
- If a creditor does not accept or misposts a payment, who corrects it and how quickly? What proof will the client receive?
- Is there a summons, judgment, garnishment, foreclosure, SSI/SSDI, Medicaid, or home-equity issue that requires a lawyer/benefits review first? The answer should be a referral—not a promise to “handle” court papers.
- Can the client cancel, how, and what happens to unspent funds? Request the answer in writing.
